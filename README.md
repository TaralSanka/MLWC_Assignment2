## Question 1 — summary of results

Five features per channel realisation: kurtosis (Eq. 2), skewness (Eq. 5), rising time
(Eq. 6), RMS delay spread (Eqs. 7–8) and Rician K-factor (Eq. 9), all computed over the
six taps of a single snapshot. Classifiers are RBF-kernel SVMs with C = 1 on a stratified
80/20 split, with `StandardScaler` fitted on the training set only.

**Part (b)** — classification accuracy (%), trained and tested at the same SNR:

| SNR (dB) | Kurtosis | Skewness | Rising time | RMS delay spread | Rician K | All five |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 59.50 | 58.75 | 79.75 | 88.50 | 60.00 | **89.25** |
| 10 | 90.75 | 93.50 | 81.25 | 93.00 | 76.00 | **99.50** |
| 20 | 93.75 | 94.50 | 83.00 | 98.25 | 81.75 | **100.00** |
| 30 | 93.75 | 95.50 | 82.50 | 98.75 | 81.50 | **100.00** |

RMS delay spread is the strongest single feature at every SNR; combining all five helps
most at 5–10 dB (+8.25 and +6.50 points over the best single feature).

**Part (c)** — a single SVM-6 trained only at 25 dB matches matched-SNR training above
~15 dB but collapses below it: 58.00% vs 89.25% at 0 dB, a 31.25-point gap.

**Appendix** — Eq. (9) approximates the K-factor as |h|²ₘₐₓ / 2σ²\_|h|. With L = 6 taps
the dominant tap inflates numerator and denominator together, so the estimator saturates
near L²/(2(L−1)) = 3.6 and the LOS mean comes out *below* the NLOS mean. The diagnostic
compares it against P_peak/(P_total − P_peak), which reaches 98% at high SNR. Parts (a)–(c)
use Eq. (9) as specified.

## Question 2 — summary of results

Square 16-QAM with I/Q from {−3, −1, 1, 3}, scaled by 1/√10 for unit average symbol
energy, AWGN of total power 1/SNR split evenly across I and Q, 200 samples per
constellation point per SNR, seed 67 — 22,400 rows in total.

**Part (b)** — at 25 dB, inertia drops off a cliff at K = 16 (49.4 → 10.2, then flat) and
the silhouette peaks there (0.880). Both select the true symbol count. The K = 16 fit
recovers the constellation with an RMS centroid error of 0.0039 against a symbol spacing
of 0.6325, giving purity 1.0000.

All three feature sets reach purity 1.0000 at 25 dB, so the required comparison is
saturated. Repeating it across SNR breaks the tie — Feature Set 1 (Cartesian) is highest
everywhere the metric is not saturated:

| SNR (dB) | Set 1 (rx_I, rx_Q) | Set 2 (r, θ) | Set 3 (all four, scaled) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **0.2547** | 0.2469 | 0.2431 |
| 10 | **0.7656** | 0.6966 | 0.6628 |
| 15 | **0.9841** | 0.9700 | 0.9678 |
| 25 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |

**Part (c)** — the fixed 25 dB centroid template beats adaptive refitting below 15 dB
(+0.0231 at 5 dB) and ties it above. At 0 dB the per-axis noise σ = 0.707 exceeds the
0.6325 symbol spacing, so adaptive centroids drift an RMS 0.5744 off the true grid — 91%
of a full symbol spacing — while the template stays pinned to the constellation and
degenerates to a minimum-distance (ML) detector.

**Appendix** — Eq. (2) of the assignment is written as arctan(rx_Q / rx_I), but
`arctan2` is used: plain `arctan` folds quadrants 3 and 4 onto 1 and 2, aliasing 8 of the
16 symbols and dropping Feature Set 2 purity from 1.0000 to 0.5188.

## Notes on implementation choices

Two places where the literal formula in the assignment was not followed, both documented
in the code and quantified by an appendix diagnostic:

1. **Q1, Rician K-factor** — Eq. (9) of the paper is implemented verbatim, even though it
   is a poor estimator at L = 6 taps. The better-behaved definition is reported alongside
   it rather than silently substituted.
2. **Q2, instantaneous phase** — `arctan2(rx_Q, rx_I)` is used instead of
   `arctan(rx_Q / rx_I)`, because the two-quadrant form makes the clustering problem
   ill-posed. The cost of the literal form is measured and reported.
